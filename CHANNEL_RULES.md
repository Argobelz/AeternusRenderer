# Tone & Voice

- Calm, intelligent, dry. Never rushed, never breathless.
- Like a smart friend explaining something at dinner, not a professor behind a podium.
- No comedy, no sensationalism, no academic lecturing.
- Honest about complexity. Always acknowledge grey areas and nuance.
- For clinical or dark psychology topics: treat with full seriousness. These are real conditions affecting real people.

---

# Wit Moments — Most Important Voice Rule

Wit moments are dry observations that make the viewer feel slightly smarter for hearing them. Use freely, as many as feel earned, but never forced. One line, then move on immediately. Never linger.

Wrong (comedy): "Confirmation bias, yep, that's you on Twitter, buddy!"

Right (wit): "Confirmation bias doesn't make you wrong about everything. It just means you'll never find out which things."

Rule: If it sounds like you're trying to be funny, cut it. If it sounds like a quiet, devastating observation, keep it.

---

# Runtime Rule

Each video runs as long as it needs to, and not one minute longer. No fixed runtime targets. No padding, no artificial extension, no compression to fit a cap. The Practical Grounding section signals the end is coming. The Outro closes.

Word count floor: 2,200 words minimum, no ceiling. Scripts go as deep as the topic demands. If a section feels thin, go deeper into the research, the nuance, the human detail, not the word count. Depth first, length follows.

---

# TTS Punctuation Rules — Qwen VoiceBox Compatibility

These apply to every script without exception.

No em dashes anywhere. Replace with a comma, a full stop, or restructure the sentence into two.

No colons anywhere. Replace with a full stop and new sentence, or restructure.

Wrong: "There are four moves: denial, diversion, trivialisation, counter-attack."

Right: "There are four moves. Denial. Diversion. Trivialisation. Counter-attack."

Wrong: "It targets the one thing you can't fact-check — what you know to be true."

Right: "It targets the one thing you can't fact-check. What you know to be true."

Every script must be checked for both characters before finalising.

---

# Outro Rule — No Cross-Video References

- Do NOT mention other videos in the outro or anywhere in the script.
- Do NOT say "next week we cover X" or tease any other video.
- Each video must stand completely alone.
- The outro closes the emotional loop of THIS video only, then makes ONE ask (subscribe).

# Anti-Template Rules — Demonetisation and Viewer Pattern Risk

YouTube flags channels with repetitive structural fingerprints. Viewers who watch multiple episodes also feel the template and lose trust in the voice. Every script must be audited against these rules before finalising. Claude is responsible for flagging these proactively. Never wait for the user to catch them.

## Hook — Never Use These Openers

- "...is one of the most..."
- "...is the oldest known..."
- Any claim about the topic's importance, prevalence, or how fascinating it is
- Any variant of "Today we're going to talk about..."
- Any framing that tells the viewer the topic matters rather than showing them

## Hook — Approved Opening Moves

Vary the opening move across every episode. No two episodes should share the same structure.

Specific Moment: Drop into a concrete scene mid-action, no setup or preamble.

Quiet Contradiction: State something true that sounds wrong.

Reframe: Name something familiar, then immediately make it strange.

Threshold Question: One genuinely unsettling question, not rhetorical hype.

Pattern Reveal: Describe a behaviour without naming it yet. Let viewers recognise themselves before they know what it's called.

Track which opening move is used per episode.

## Outro — Never Do These

- Generic "now you understand this exists" wrap
- Same emotional register every episode
- Templated phrases like "And that's why this matters" or "So next time you see this..."
- Same subscribe ask phrasing repeated across episodes

## Outro Standards

- Must close the emotional loop opened in THAT episode's hook specifically
- Vary emotional register across episodes: unease / quiet resolution / provocation / stillness
- ONE subscribe ask per episode, in varied language

## Transition Anti-Patterns

- Audit each script for repeated bridge phrases between sections
- Common offenders: "But here's where it gets interesting" / "And that brings us to" / "Which is where it gets complicated"
- If the same phrase appears across multiple episodes, rewrite at least one

# Script Structure (8 Sections)

Every Aeternus video follows this structure. Sections expand as the content requires. No fixed word counts per section.

1. Hook. Open cold. No intro, no welcome back, no title card. Specific scenario, then unsettling question, then payoff promise.
2. Framing. Set up the intellectual framework. Tell the viewer how to think about what's coming.
3. Core Section 1. What it actually is, how it presents, what drives it, nuance most sources miss.
4. Core Section 2. The most misunderstood dimension.
5. Core Section 3. The most complex or morally nuanced angle. Can expand to 4 or 5 core sections when the topic warrants it.
6. Insight Layer. The deeper connection, the pattern, the reframe that makes everything click differently.
7. Practical Grounding. Not a self-help listicle. A calm, honest answer to: so what do I do with this? Close without preaching.
8. Outro. Close the emotional loop from the Hook. ONE subscribe ask. No teases, no cross-references.

# High-Retention and Demonetisation Standards

Every script must be built for viewer retention and platform safety, not just content quality.

## Retention

- Every section must end with a reason to keep watching. Not a cliffhanger gimmick, but a genuine open thread, an unresolved question, a complexity that hasn't been addressed yet.
- The Framing section must make the viewer feel that what's coming is worth the full length of the video. If they could leave after Framing and feel satisfied, rewrite it.
- Core sections must contain at least one specific, concrete detail per section, a real case, a named study, a documented example. Abstraction without grounding loses viewers.
- The Insight Layer must genuinely reframe what came before. If it only summarises, it loses the viewer who was already tracking. It has to change the angle.

## Demonetisation Risk

- No gratuitous detail on methods of harm, violence, or self-harm. Describe impact and psychology, not mechanics.
- Sensitive topics (abuse, mental illness, suicide-adjacent content) must include appropriate framing and the pinned comment must carry support signposting.
- No extreme political framing or partisan language. Aeternus covers human behaviour, not political sides.
- Avoid sensationalist language anywhere in the script, title, description, or tags. Calm authority is the brand. Alarm is not.
- Titles must be curiosity-led, not fear-led. "How X works" outperforms "The terrifying truth about X" for long-term channel health.

# Script Deliverable Format (5 Required Sections)

Every script must include all five.

1. Full Script. Complete voiceover following the 8-section structure with clear section headers.
2. YouTube Title and Description. Title formula: [Topic] then [Curiosity gap or reframe]. Full description with bullet preview, disclaimer where relevant, chapter timestamps, no cross-video references.
3. Tags. 20 to 30 YouTube tags.
4. Pinned Comment. Warm, responsible. Include support signposting for sensitive topics. End with a question that invites comments.
5. Thumbnail AI Prompt. Painterly oil painting style, character pose and expression, scene mood, colour palette, lighting, text overlay suggestion.
